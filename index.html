Network

What the network does

Proof-of-Stake

Blocks are minted by holders staking TRI. The reward is 33% per coin-year. No mining hardware, no electricity arms race, and rewards compound automatically.

Hash9

The consensus hash is a 13-step cascade designed in July 2014. Proof-of-work ended at block 9,000, and the network has been pure proof-of-stake ever since.

Encrypted messaging

End-to-end encrypted peer-to-peer messages (smsg) are part of the protocol, not a separate app. No servers, no phone numbers; both clients carry it.

Tor and I2P routing

Connections are anonymized per peer address: .onion peers through Tor, .b32.i2p peers through I2P. The classic wallet compiles both in and makes no clearnet connections by default; tri7 additionally speaks clearnet and bundles Tor on Windows.

Signed fast bootstrap

Classic-wallet UTXO snapshots are signed with an Ed25519 key published in the manifest, and the wallet verifies the signature before applying one.

Compact blocks and fork detection

Classic-wallet BIP152 short-ID relay cuts bandwidth on slow links, and a background thread compares your chain tip against the peer median every 60 seconds.

Staking

Stake by holding

Hold TRI in a wallet, leave it unlocked for staking, and the client does the rest. There is no minimum amount and no lock-up; your keys stay on your machine and rewards compound automatically.

Privacy

Privacy, stated carefully

The project ships no telemetry, runs no analytics, and has no accounts. Addresses are not linked to identity, and there is no server that could do the linking.

Network privacy depends on the transport. The classic wallet routes every peer connection through Tor v3 or I2P by default, so your IP address is not exposed to the peers you talk to; advanced users can override this (-nontor). tri7 speaks clearnet as well as Tor and I2P, so on tri7 anonymity is a property of which network your peers use; privacy-minded users should prefer its Tor and I2P peers. Neither client is a VPN or a mixer, and no anonymity tool makes guarantees absolute.

Every claim on this page can be checked against the published source code.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Which client should I use?

Both are full nodes for the same chain. If you want the newest experience, meaning seed phrases, auto-updates, a built-in explorer, and a small daemon, use tri7. If you want the original Qt wallet, the strict Tor/I2P-only default, or a package-manager install, use the classic client. Run them side by side on separate machines if you like; don't point two staking clients at the same wallet file.

Is tri7 a different coin or a fork?

No. tri7 is a reimplementation in Rust of the same full-node rules, replaying the same chain block for block. It stakes and spends the same TRI and does not create a new network or a new asset.

Is Triangles a fork of Bitcoin or Monero?

No. Triangles has its own consensus algorithm (Hash9) and its own codebase, written from scratch in 2014. The classic client is C++ with a Qt interface; tri7 is Rust with an egui interface. Both are released under the MIT license.

How is 33% staking sustainable?

The rate is new TRI issued per year as a fraction of staked supply, documented in the source. As more TRI stakes, the per-staker share dilutes. Supply is capped at 2,222,222 TRI, so issuance is finite.

What does “Tor by default” mean for the classic wallet?

Every peer connection is wrapped in an anonymity circuit: Tor v3 for .onion peers, the embedded I2P router for .b32.i2p peers. Your IP address is not exposed to the peers you transact with. If I2P fails to bootstrap, the wallet falls back to Tor-only operation rather than connecting in the clear. Advanced users can override this (-nontor), which is why the claims here are scoped to the default.

Why I2P in addition to Tor?

They are different anonymity networks with different designs: Tor favors low-latency client connections, while I2P favors peer-to-peer services with unidirectional tunnels. Triangles speaks both. The classic client embeds both routers in one binary; tri7 dials either through its bundled or local daemons. A peer is reached over whichever network its address uses.

Is the encrypted messaging a separate app?

No. It is built into both clients: add a contact by TRI address and message over the same peer-to-peer network the chain uses. In tri7 the outbox is also readable with your sending key.

Is this project still active?

Yes. tri7 v7.0.3 shipped November 11, 2026, and the classic wallet v6.2.6.7 shipped September 7, 2026. The explorer is live, releases have shipped on a steady cadence through 2026, and both repositories take issues and pull requests.

How do I verify the binary I downloaded?

Check its SHA-256 against the values in the Download section. The checksums confirm the download is intact and unmodified; they are not a proof of how it was built. To go further, clone the repository, check out the release tag, and build it yourself: triangles_v5 and tri7 both include build instructions.
